Dashboard ⚖️ Supply & Demand 18 Nov 2026
The one-line version

Make more of what the audience already watches, and less of what it skips.

Demand is what viewers watch on YouTube, and what professionals and businesses pay for. Supply is what we can make each week: about 10.5 production hours, 3 Shorts, 1 long video and a live clinic. This page sets the two side by side and says what to do about the gap.

↩️ Where I went in the wrong direction

I built pages before the core message existed.

The audience map, sprint, and reverse-response pages each had their own framing. The core message should have come first and every page should have hung off it.

I set dates before the start date was decided.

The calendar is still two days out (IN-8). I removed the Mondays and kept countdowns running against dates you haven't confirmed.

I wrote reverse-response plans with no real target.

The response cards say "fast-growing video" but nobody has picked one. I should have asked for the list before building the page.

I treated the goal pace as a fact.

The 15 subscribers a week is my assumption. The timeline is only as good as that number, and it hasn't been measured.

I assumed Shorts were the weak format and didn't ask how they were made.

The average of 99 views doesn't separate Flow Shorts from filmed ones. I should have flagged that the comparison was mixed.

I wrote the Cambridge shoot into the plan as a real walk.

The Days 2–7 footage is generated, not filmed of you. The sprint now says so, but I should have asked before writing "walk".

I claimed things I had not verified.

The Azure content endpoint returned 401 in my test and I didn't confirm the save path end to end. I should have said so at the time.

🧭 How to course correct

  1. 1
    Make the core message the source of truth.

    Every card, page and video answers to the core message page. If a piece doesn't serve it, cut it.

  2. 2
    Export the Studio data this week.

    Compare Flow and filmed videos on watch time. Only then decide how much Flow supply to add.

  3. 3
    Move supply toward demand.

    Add developer-tooling videos (demand 383, supply 1). Cut one RAG Short (demand 205, supply 3).

  4. 4
    Confirm the start date and the Cambridge footage label.

    Two answers from you. Then the countdowns and the production notes are correct.

  5. 5
    Replace my assumptions with logged numbers.

    Hours per video, Skool members, subscribers gained. Re-run the goal timeline from those, not from 15 a week.

  6. 6
    Start tracking business enquiries.

    Add one line to the weekly log: managed-services enquiries, even if zero. Without it the third audience is invisible.

  7. 7
    Check every four weeks.

    Repeat this table with new numbers. If a topic's share of views moves more than 5 points, move supply the same way.
